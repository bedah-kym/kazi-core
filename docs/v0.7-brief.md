# Kazi Core v0.7 — Product Brief: The teammate you can name

**Status:** shipped in 0.7.0 (2026-10-04). Handoffs and the routine test run shipped as runtime only, with no chat or UI entry point yet; the egress deferral in §9 was overtaken by the opt-in egress proxy.
**Owner:** Bedan
**Extends:** `v0.6-brief.md` (learning foundation §5, decision log §9)
**Recasts:** the "God-tier workflows" epic (#160). The workflow upgrades remain the
artifacts; this brief is the product shape around them.

---

## 0. The one-sentence story

> **In v0.6 Kazi got hands. In v0.7 it gets a name and a job — Jon teaches it
> once, approves the routine once, and stops building workflows entirely.**

### Jon, six months in

Jon used to open the workflow screen about as often as he changed his router
password. Now he doesn't open it at all. He says: *"watch the NAS and tell me
before it fills"* — that's a watch. A month later he walks Kazi through the
Friday backup check once: *"save that as a skill."* Kazi drafts it, Jon fixes one
step, approves the routine, and the standing grant that lets it run on Fridays.
Every run leaves a receipt. When the backup script changes shape, the grant
lapses — Kazi goes back to asking. When Jon's away for two weeks, Kazi asks once
whether to keep things running; no answer means they pause.

What Jon never did: hand-build a workflow, write a connector, or grant a blanket
"do whatever" permission. **The skill is legible. The routine is owned. The
grant is scoped, measured, and revocable.**

---

## 1. Why v0.7 (and why this shape)

v0.6 answered "can Kazi *act*?" with a governed shell, a persistent workspace,
and a learning foundation (L1 telemetry, L2 preference mining, L3 initiative
ladder). v0.7 answers the next question: **how does a normal person keep an
agent on a job for months?**

The field answered that question first, and we should be honest about the
benchmark. Grok Bot (docs.x.ai/grok-bot) ships the user-facing idiom we are
missing:

- durable **named bots** with roles and standing rules,
- **skills** — reusable instructions with an explicit contract, learned from
  demonstration, saved as a draft before they run,
- **routines** — a bot owns a schedule/event, an input source, an output, a
  no-data policy, a test run before enabling, and run history,
- **approvals** — allow once / always allow (a saved, narrow rule) / deny, with
  "ask first" winning conflicts,
- **handoffs** — bots pass work to each other with one owner per stage,
- and a shared persistent **computer** where context compounds.

That validates the v0.6 seam (sandbox + approval + receipts) and tells us what
to build on top of it. What Grok Bot does not have — and what remains Kazi's
moat — is the depth of the governance: versioned definitions, a deterministic
capability manifest, per-rule standing grants that **lapse on drift**, an
append-only receipt trail, and self-hosting on Jon's own box.

**Borrow the idiom. Keep the moat.**

## 2. What already exists (the foundation)

v0.7 is an assembly job, not a rewrite:

- `skills/` registry — `SKILL.md` folders with `staging | review | active`
  gates, `list_skills` / `load_skill` meta-tools (`skill_registry.py`).
- `WorkflowDraft` → `UserWorkflow`; triggers (webhook/schedule/manual);
  `WorkflowExecution` with receipts, replay-safety, denials, failure summaries;
  idempotency keys.
- `WorkflowApprovalRecord` — durable approvals, two kinds, expiring.
- `WorkflowImprovementSuggestion` — the small rules-engine ancestor of the
  reviewer agent.
- L1–L3: `telemetry_rollups.py`, `preference_mining.py`, `initiative.py`
  (digest, proposals, approved rules, proactive budget).
- Governed shell: sandbox profiles, per-command network, persistent workspace,
  snapshot/rollback, two-tier escalation.
- `delegate_task` sub-agents with hard budget caps; append-only receipts;
  `kazi_trace`; the golden eval harness.

Missing pieces this cycle adds: versioning, a skill contract, a routine
contract, standing grants with lapse, named personas, internal handoffs, and a
skill lifecycle.

## 3. The primitives

### 3.1 Skill — *how*

A skill is a reviewable instruction artifact with a fixed contract:

1. **When to use it**
2. **Required inputs and access**
3. **The sequence of work**
4. **How to validate the result**
5. **What to return**
6. **What requires approval**

Skills are drafts until a human promotes them. Promotion reuses the existing
`skills/` stages (`staging` → `review` → `active`); the machine-readable
definition lives in a versioned workflow (#155) and the prose contract is the
skill body. The explicit path — *"save what we just did as a skill"* — packages
the session's tool sequence into a `WorkflowDraft` plus a staged skill folder
(#156). The statistical path mines repeated patterns and queues candidates; it
never creates anything active.

### 3.2 Routine — *when*

A routine binds a skill to a schedule or event and must answer seven questions
before it can be enabled (#204):

1. **Owner** — the persona that owns it and reports failures (#203).
2. **Inputs** — sources, and how freshness is verified.
3. **Output** — expected result, and where it is delivered.
4. **Approval boundary** — default: send / purchase / delete / publish /
   production changes all require approval.
5. **No-data / stale-data policy** — refuse and report; never silently run on
   stale data.
6. **Partial-completion policy** — where partial results are reported.
7. **Idempotency** — retries reuse the existing `idempotency_key` path.

A routine cannot be enabled under a standing grant until a **test run** passes
(§3.3). After a long absence, Kazi asks once whether to keep routines running;
no answer means they pause. The last K runs are visible next to the routine.

### 3.3 Standing grant — *may*

"Permission once" done right (#159), with the benchmark's semantics:

- **Allow once** / **Always allow** (saves a matching rule) / **Deny**.
- A grant is scoped to `(workflow version, trigger, action/resource scope)` —
  never a global unlock.
- A **wider capability** (`capability_delta > 0`, #167) cannot be
  "always allowed"; it re-enters approval.
- **Ask first wins** over any allow rule when both match.
- **Lapse conditions**: new action/resource outside the grant, a failure spike,
  or N days elapsed — on lapse the routine re-gates and Jon re-approves.
- Each lapse reason and active grant is visible in the ops UI; every autonomous
  run writes a receipt.

### 3.4 Persona — *who*

A named specialist with a job description, a tool/connector scope, a risk
ceiling, and an approval boundary (#203). Created like a workflow (draft →
confirmed), by chat or duplication. Scope and ceiling are enforced
deterministically in the tool path — an out-of-scope call is **denied**, not
prompted. A persona can never exceed its human's access; there is no machine
identity.

### 3.5 Handoff — *with*

An internal, visible handoff with one owner per stage (#137, re-scoped):
task + context refs + working scope + budget in; result + artifacts + receipts
out. Built on the existing `delegate_task` machinery. No external runtime, no
#125 spike. A handoff is never hidden from the human.

## 4. The trust doctrine (draft-first)

Codified from the benchmark's "design routines for trust", applied to every
draft, promotion, and reviewer suggestion above:

1. **Automate preparation before execution.** Draft, reconcile, or recommend
   first.
2. **Sending, purchasing, deleting, publishing, and production changes require
   approval** — by default, not by configuration.
3. **Every routine has a no-data and stale-data policy.** Stale output is a
   failure, not a result.
4. **Retries are idempotent** where possible (existing idempotency keys).
5. **Partial completion is reported** to a named place.
6. **Re-test after the source changes.** A routine pauses when its assumed
   input shape drifts; the reviewer proposes the fix.
7. **Approval cards show target, scope, and effect** — `preview()` effects
   (#168) and the capability delta (#167), not raw parameters.
8. **Memory defers to the source for consequential decisions.** L1 facts carry
   a freshness marker; the reviewer cites current metrics, not memory.

## 5. Non-goals

Hard lines, not preferences:

- **No cloud computer, no browser, no computer-use.** Kazi's reach stays the
  governed shell and connectors. Teach-by-demonstration is not this cycle.
- **No external-runtime delegation.** #125 stays parked; #137 is internal now.
- **No global autonomy unlock.** Every grant is per-rule, lapsing, and
  receipted. Nothing self-authorizes.
- **No core self-fix.** The agent loop, tool executor, and security policy stay
  PR-only, forever.
- **No marketplace or sharing.** The skill library is local to one install;
  community distribution is a later question.
- **No multi-agent framework.** Personas and handoffs are bounded: one owner
  per stage, budgets enforced, no free-running swarms.
- **No agent-loop or tool-executor contract changes** this cycle, except the
  persona scope check and the existing risk-gate hooks (both need a plan and a
  human OK).

## 6. Build order

Do not jump ahead. Each rung depends on the one above it.

1. **#155 W-A** — workflow definition versioning. Everything below binds to
   versions.
2. **#167 W-A2** — capability manifest + `capability_delta`. Deterministic
   widening check.
3. **#156 W-B** *(amended)* — promotion pipeline: explicit "save this as a
   skill", statistical candidates, the §3.1 contract on every draft.
4. **#204 W-F** — routine contract (§3.2), test-run gate, pause-on-absence,
   run history.
5. **#159 W-E** *(amended)* — standing grants (§3.3) + #166 approval telemetry.
6. **#157 W-C** *(amended)* — health watches, auto-pause (pause only, never
   edit), weekly digest, healthy/degraded/paused.
7. **#158 W-D** + **#169 W-D2** *(amended)* — reviewer agent; shadow replay
   positioned as the routine **test run**; the reviewer model differs from the
   author model.
8. **#203 W-G** — agent personas (§3.4).
9. **#137 W-G2** — internal specialist handoffs (§3.5).
10. **#138** *(unblocked)* — skill lifecycle: candidate → staging → review →
    active, with stale/archived states and pinning.

Red lines from epic #160 are unchanged: no in-place definition edits, no core
self-fix, permission-once is per-rule, nothing self-authorizes.

## 7. Success metrics

- A task Jon repeats becomes a **proposed skill and routine** without Jon
  building anything; every promotion traces to the approval he gave once.
- A routine cannot be enabled until a **test run** passes and its no-data
  policy exists.
- A **standing grant** covers exactly its scope; a widening change re-gates;
  a drift lapse returns the routine to asking.
- A failing workflow **pauses itself** before Jon notices, and says why.
- A reviewer suggestion ships with **evidence** (shadow diff + cited metric),
  and no suggestion reaches Jon without a test run.
- A handoff has **one owner per stage**, a receipt, and is visible in the
  originating conversation.

### What success does *not* look like

- "Kazi runs everything automatically now" — fail. Grants are scoped and
  lapse.
- "We shipped a workflow builder" — fail. Setup is a message; the artifacts
  stay behind the curtain.
- "The agent approved itself" — fail. Every promotion routes through the
  durable human seam.
- "The core improved itself" — fail. PR-only.
- "We copied Grok Bot's cloud" — fail. Borrow the idiom, keep the moat.

## 8. Decision log

- **Grok-Bot-shaped v0.7.** The product primitives are skills, routines,
  standing grants, personas, and handoffs; `UserWorkflow` is the artifact
  underneath, not the interface.
- **Workflow versioning is the foundation.** No self-improvement without
  versioned definitions (#155 first).
- **Test run before enable, always.** Shadow replay (#169) is the evidence
  gate for routines and reviewer suggestions.
- **Standing grants follow the benchmark semantics**: allow once / always
  allow / deny; ask-first wins; widening cannot be auto-allowed; drift lapses.
- **Delegation is internal.** #137 re-scoped to specialist handoffs; the
  external-runtime spike (#125) is parked.
- **Procedural memory is unblocked** (#138) as the skill lifecycle, building on
  W-A/W-B, not a separate curator.
- **Draft-first is doctrine**, enforced through acceptance criteria, not
  prompt text.

## 9. Open questions

1. **True per-host egress firewall** — still deferred (v0.6 §11); the standing
   grant makes the gap more painful but does not change the answer this cycle.
2. **Remote-exec backend** — diagnosing other machines. Still deferred; the
   persona model is the natural place to attach it later.
3. **Windows/PowerShell profile** — still bash-first.
4. **Browser/computer-use reach** — explicitly not this cycle; if it ever
   lands, it enters through the same sandbox + approval seam, never beside it.
5. **Skill marketplace / sharing** — a bot template is safe to share; a skill
   with workspace access is not. Decide after the local lifecycle (#138) has
   real content.
6. **A model-based review layer** (the benchmark's Auto Review) — interesting,
   but Kazi's doctrine is deterministic enforcement. Revisit only as a
   *proposal* layer on top of the hard controls, never as a boundary.
